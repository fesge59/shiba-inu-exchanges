## What you're actually buying

Shiba Inu (SHIB) is an ERC-20 token on Ethereum, launched in August 2020 by an anonymous developer using the name Ryoshi. It arrived with a supply of roughly one quadrillion tokens, which is why the unit price sits in fractions of a cent and why a "10x" on SHIB means very small numbers moving a long way. Its all-time high was around $0.00008845, and it has traded far below that since.

The ecosystem around it is real, even if the token started as a joke: there's a DEX (ShibaSwap), a Layer-2 network (Shibarium), and companion tokens. None of that changes the risk profile — this is a meme coin whose price leans heavily on community interest and sentiment rather than cash flows. Buy it with money you can afford to see drop 80%.

## The three places you can actually buy SHIB

**Centralized exchanges.** Gate, Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX, Bybit and most others list SHIB. You register, pass identity verification, fund the account, and place a spot order. Fiat on-ramps and customer support exist. The trade-off: the exchange holds the keys, and you hand over personal documents.

**Decentralized exchanges.** Uniswap and similar AMMs on Ethereum let you swap ETH (or a stablecoin) for SHIB straight from a self-custody wallet. No account, no KYC. The costs are less friendly than they look: a typical pool fee of around 0.3%, plus Ethereum gas, which can easily exceed the value of a small SHIB purchase. There's also no customer service if you fat-finger a contract address.

**Peer-to-peer, inside an exchange.** Gate's P2P/C2C market (also called C2C on some platforms) lets you buy from other users at an agreed price, with the platform holding the crypto in escrow until the fiat leg clears. Gate charges no platform fee on C2C; the seller sets the price, so compare quotes before accepting one.

For a first SHIB purchase of a few hundred dollars, a centralized exchange is almost always the cheaper and less error-prone route.

## Step by step

1. **Register.** Email or phone number, then set up two-factor authentication in the security settings. Use an authenticator app rather than SMS — SIM-swap attacks are a real thing and exchanges can't reverse an authorised withdrawal.
2. **Complete KYC.** Upload a photo ID and pass the facial-recognition check for the basic tier.
3. **Fund the account.** Bank transfer or card if you're bringing in fiat; a crypto deposit if you already hold USDT. Card and SEPA Instant transfers usually land within seconds to minutes, other bank transfers can take about a business day.
4. **Buy SHIB.** Go to spot trading and find SHIB/USDT. A market order fills immediately at the best available price; a limit order lets you name your price and pay the maker fee instead of the taker fee. If you'd rather not think about order types, use Flash Swap instead.
5. **Check the fill.** Funds sit in your spot wallet under Assets. The confirmation screen shows the total fee before you commit — read it.
6. **Decide where it lives.** Small positions you plan to trade can stay on the exchange. Anything you intend to hold long-term belongs in a wallet you control.

## The parts Gate doesn't do well

Worth knowing before you commit:

- **Regional availability is patchy.** Gate's international platform isn't offered to US residents. The group runs separate entities for different markets — a MiCA authorisation via Malta passported across the EEA, a Japan FSA registration, a Dubai VARA licence, and an AUSTRAC registration in Australia — but the catalogue and products available to you depend on which entity holds your account. Check eligibility before you start.
- **The primary international entity is offshore.** Independent reviewers put Gate around 7.8/10 overall and flag the same thing every time: the Cayman-domiciled entity sits under VASP registration rather than a top-tier prudential regulator, and there's no statutory compensation scheme behind it. That's not disqualifying, but it should change how much you leave on the exchange.
- **Support is live chat and email only.** No phone line. Third-party testing has measured median first-response times of a few minutes on chat, with emailed escalations running to several hours and KYC reviews stretching to 24–48 hours during busy periods.
- **Withdrawal fees move.** They're recalculated hourly against network conditions. A TRC-20 USDT withdrawal is cheap and fast; an ERC-20 withdrawal on a congested day is neither.

On the plus side: Gate has published Merkle-tree proof-of-reserves snapshots monthly since 2020, and a June 2026 third-party review cites a self-reported 115% reserve ratio backed by zero-knowledge proofs covering roughly $8.18 billion. Self-reported isn't audited, but the cadence is better than most.

## After you buy: custody decisions

**Leaving it on the exchange** is fine for a position you're actively trading or planning to sell soon. Enable 2FA, whitelist your withdrawal addresses, and don't reuse passwords.

**Moving it to your own wallet** is the right call for anything you plan to hold. SHIB is an ERC-20 token, so you'll need an Ethereum-compatible wallet, and you'll pay gas to move it. If you plan to use Shibarium or ShibaSwap, check which networks the wallet supports before you send anything.

Two scam patterns worth naming, because SHIB attracts more of them than most tokens: fake "SHIB" tokens on Ethereum with similar names and symbols, and giveaways promising free SHIB if you connect your wallet. Verify the contract address through an official source, and treat any DM containing a link as hostile by default.
